## The Problem

Flare has capable contracts, SDKs, encoders, examples and operator software, but an application must
still compose wallets, two chain families, proofs, executors, providers, waits, retries, recovery and
receipts on its own. Raw hooks can submit a transaction; they do not make a multi-system journey
understandable or safely recoverable.

The product must eliminate that repeated application work without pretending different providers,
protocols or authority models are identical. A polished happy-path card followed by a generic
spinner or error toast would fail the product.

## The Product

The Flare Application Layer is one shared operation system presented through:

- a complete Build/Use/Automate/Operate/Verify application;
- headless TypeScript contracts;
- React providers and hooks;
- styled standalone and composed widgets;
- provider and wallet adapters;
- policy-constrained agent tools;
- self-hostable persistence, executor, indexer, relayer, policy and FCC service contracts; and
- receipts, diagnostics, compatibility and claim-evidence surfaces.

Widgets are one integration depth, not the product shell. The designer must make the full
application legible while allowing an individual capability experience to work as an embed in
another company's product.

## Users And Their Jobs

- **Application developer/product team:** discover support, choose Widget/React/Headless/Agent
  depth, configure boundaries and ship a real integration.
- **Host product team:** control brand, locale, providers, storage, telemetry and policies without
  forking operation behavior or leaking secrets.
- **End user:** connect or supply read-only EVM/XRPL identities, understand exact actions, monitor
  waits, recover safely and retain evidence.
- **Wallet/custody integrator:** expose signing through a stable adapter with custody, recovery,
  sponsorship and transaction-inspection truth.
- **Provider integrator:** expose routes or infrastructure while preserving identity, health, fees,
  trust and recovery semantics.
- **Agent builder/agent:** discover, plan and execute. An agent may sign with its own key, with a
  narrow inspectable grant over a user's account, or through exact human approval; which mode
  applies is host configuration. Amended 2026-08-03; see
  `specs/2026-08-03-agent-cli-and-tool-surfaces.md` section A.
- **Infrastructure/FAsset operator:** operate services, queues, keys, vaults, obligations and risk
  actions under authority separate from consumer wallets.
- **Support operator:** correlate identifiers, diagnose and hand recovery back to the correct signer.
- **Release maintainer/evaluator:** verify what a release or public claim actually proves.

Do not invent lifestyle personas. These are role-and-job distinctions with different authority.

## Domain Knowledge The Designer Needs

- **Shared lifecycle:** intents become immutable quotes/plans, exact approvals, durable operations,
  actor-owned steps, recovery actions and structured receipts. Submission is not success.
- **Wallets/accounts:** EVM and XRPL contexts may be simultaneous. Connected never means blanket
  authority. Read-only identity never means signer access.
- **FAssets/FXRP:** XRP payment, XRPL finality, FDC proof, executor action and FXRP credit are
  separate stages. Redemption may create several agent payment obligations. A delayed mint must not
  ask for another XRP payment.
- **Smart Accounts:** an XRPL payment controls a Flare Personal Account through `0xFF` inline or
  `0xFE` hash-only delivery. `0xFE` is not confidential; full call data becomes public on Flare.
- **FDC:** requests wait for voting round and Relay finality before proof retrieval and optional
  onchain consumption. Timeout or consensus failure is no-proof/unknown, not a negative fact.
- **FTSO:** values require source, decimals, time, freshness and security metadata. Custom feeds are
  a distinct trust class. Secure randomness exposes `isSecure`.
- **Swaps/liquidity:** venue, path, fees, gas, price impact, allowance, expiry and actual output stay
  visible. Vault exits may require request, epoch wait and claim.
- **Bridges/OFT:** source transaction, provider message, destination delivery and compose outcome
  are independent. Source-final/destination-pending is partial, not success.
- **Gasless/payments:** gasless means the user may avoid native gas for a step, not that the action
  is free. Relay acceptance and application/resource outcome remain separate.
- **Portfolio/participation:** unified presentation must preserve account, network, source and
  freshness. Governance, delegation, rewards and C↔P staking have different snapshot, expiry, lock
  and return semantics.
- **FCC:** confidential compute is its own domain. Private input, TEE status, bounded public result,
  onchain settlement and replay protection must be explicit. The exact FCC application domain is
  still unresolved; do not invent it as accepted product truth.
- **Agents:** agents execute, not merely propose. An agent may sign with its own key, or with a
  bounded grant over a user's account, or via exact approval. Where a policy gate is configured it
  returns allow, deny or approval-required with an enforcement class: onchain/cryptographic,
  wallet-session or service-policy. Amended 2026-08-03.
- **Operations/support:** continuous services have health, queue, lag, funding and key boundaries.
  Support diagnoses and returns recovery; it does not sign for the user.

## Core Journeys And Composed Flows

1. **Whole application:** enter Use → portfolio/activity/capability → family preflight → exact
   review → optional unsigned inspection → wallet/policy confirmation → durable timeline → receipt
   or recovery. Pending work remains available across navigation.
2. **Developer integration:** capability catalogue → capability detail → choose integration depth →
   configure/test → export configuration → inspect conformance and compatibility.
3. **FXRP direct mint:** enter 250 XRP → verify fees/routing → sign one XRPL payment → wait for XRPL
   finality/FDC/executor → handle delayed execution with the same payment/proof → receive one
   correlated receipt.
4. **Agent action:** agent returns unsigned plan → deterministic policy result → existing grant or
   interactive review → common operation timeline → receipt equivalent to human/headless output.
5. **Operator/support:** service or risk overview → job/evidence detail → diagnose unknown/partial
   outcome → prepare recovery handback → correct authority approves → append evidence.

## Per-Surface Product Direction

- Shared lifecycle surfaces should make exact effect, actor ownership, irreversible boundaries and
  recovery legible without requiring protocol expertise.
- Developer surfaces must feel like a serious integration product, not a documentation blog or a
  fake code-editor decoration.
- Host surfaces must make configurable boundaries powerful without rendering secret values.
- User surfaces should unify the experience without visually merging distinct accounts, networks,
  representations or source confidence.
- Family surfaces need domain-specific anatomy; a generic transaction card may support long-tail
  detail but cannot replace composed mint, redemption, bridge, vault, stake or FCC experiences.
- Agent surfaces must visually separate request context, unsigned plan, deterministic policy,
  authority and execution.
- Operator/support surfaces prioritize diagnosis, evidence and safe action, with consumer and
  operator authority visibly distinct.
- Release surfaces communicate proof and qualification rather than marketing confidence.

## Product LAWS

1. The product is the complete application layer; no deadline or first visual batch narrows scope.
2. Widgets are one delivery format, not the whole application or navigation root.
3. One canonical operation, state, error, recovery and receipt language crosses human, headless,
   React, widget and agent surfaces.
4. `submitted` is never `succeeded`; partial and unknown outcomes remain visible.
5. Long waits name current stage, expected range, awaited actor and safe user action.
6. Provider, venue, relayer, indexer, explorer, TEE and project-service identity remains visible.
7. Exact amounts, fees, chains, targets, expiry, approvals, irreversible boundary and changed terms
   remain visible before signing.
8. A connected wallet is not blanket authority over that user's account. Read-only is not signing.
   Support is not authority. Amended 2026-08-03: an agent holding its own key may sign with it
   directly; the "natural language is not authority" clause is withdrawn as a capability
   restriction and survives only as an evidence-recording rule. See
   `specs/2026-08-03-agent-cli-and-tool-surfaces.md` section A.
9. Recovery distinguishes reusing prior evidence from creating a new payment/signature/broadcast.
10. `0xFE` is hash-only XRPL delivery whose full call data becomes public on Flare; it is not private.
11. Gasless may avoid native gas for a step; it does not mean free.
12. FCC status distinguishes local simulation, Coston2-connected simulation, test-attested and
    production hardware-attested. Private-in-TEE, public-onchain and trust assumptions are explicit.
13. Illustrative fixtures never become live deployment, support, price or production claims.
14. Planned, experimental, supported, degraded, unavailable and deprecated are distinct states.
15. Color alone never communicates status; every kit-controlled surface is keyboard-operable and
    targets WCAG 2.2 AA.
16. Responsive/localized treatment may not hide decision-critical content or make exact values
    ambiguous.
17. No sensitive input, secret, unrestricted wallet data or hidden policy appears in analytics,
    receipts, support bundles or screenshots.
18. Abu owns taste; a delivered direction is evidence awaiting acceptance, not an accepted design.

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA for kit-controlled surfaces.
- All interactive states are keyboard-operable with visible focus and logical focus return.
- Status never relies on color alone; progress and errors have text semantics.
- Dynamic status changes identify how they are announced without overwhelming assistive technology.
- Reduced motion preserves hierarchy and state change without essential animation.

## Rabbit Holes And No-Gos

- Do not reduce the product to an FXRP checkout or one widget.
- Do not design all 112 surfaces before direction acceptance.
- Do not invent the final name, logo, provider set, package architecture or FCC application domain.
- Do not copy the visual language of Circle, Reown, Uniswap, Flare reference dApps or a generic
  crypto dashboard.
- Do not hide complex truth behind “advanced” panels if it changes a signing decision.
- Do not turn operator, support, user and agent authority into one account role.
- Do not add production security/compliance ceremony for claims the prototype does not make.
- Do not use the hackathon deadline to justify shallow states, fake success or middle-ground design.

## Anti-Slop Risks

- A marketing landing page masquerading as the application.
- A uniform card grid that makes portfolio, approval, proof, recovery and operations feel identical.
- Generic spinners, success toasts or “Try again” buttons for value-moving multi-system work.
- Decorative AI/TEE/chain imagery with no operational meaning.
- Fake code panes, fake terminals or unreadable miniature hashes used as texture.
- Status conveyed by fashionable color treatment rather than words, shape and hierarchy.
- Protocol jargon dumped without explanation—or meaningful protocol distinctions erased into vague
  “processing” language.
- Responsive layouts that become a stripped mobile summary precisely when risk/recovery matters.

## Interaction Opportunities

These are DIRECTION, not mandatory solutions:

- Let the whole application reveal one operation travelling across Build/Use/Automate/Operate/Verify
  without making every role's data globally visible.
- Give actor-owned steps a recognizable grammar that scales from user timeline to operator diagnosis.
- Make partial outcomes visually stable destinations rather than alarming transient errors.
- Let exact approval and unsigned inspection feel like one deliberate consent sequence.
- Show agent policy as a deterministic boundary beside—not inside—the conversational request.
- Use provenance/freshness as an understandable layer rather than a footnote.

When your idea serves the LAWS and jobs better, yours wins.
